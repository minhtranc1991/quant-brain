# Chapter 04 — Public Works Mean Taxes

## Summary

Hazlitt applies the same seen/unseen logic to public works spending: a government bridge or building is visible, but the taxes that funded it represent private spending and investment that never happened and is therefore unseen. He argues public works are justified only when the project itself is worth more than what taxpayers would otherwise have produced with the same resources — not merely because it "creates jobs," since taxation destroys as many jobs elsewhere as the spending creates.
