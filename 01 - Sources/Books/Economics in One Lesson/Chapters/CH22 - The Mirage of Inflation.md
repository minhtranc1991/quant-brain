## Summary

Hazlitt argues that monetary inflation (expanding the money supply) creates only an illusion of prosperity: nominal prices, wages, and profits rise, but real output does not increase merely because the currency unit is worth less, and inflation redistributes real wealth arbitrarily between creditors/debtors and fixed-income earners rather than creating any new wealth, while also distorting relative prices and capital calculation during the adjustment period.
