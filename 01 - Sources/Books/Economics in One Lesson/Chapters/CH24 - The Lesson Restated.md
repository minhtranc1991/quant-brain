## Summary

Hazlitt closes by restating the book's single lesson (Chapter 1) and reviewing how every preceding chapter applied it: economic policy must be judged by tracing its effects on all groups over the long run, not merely its immediate, visible effect on one group, because special-interest pleading and short-run thinking are the persistent, self-renewing sources of economic fallacy.
