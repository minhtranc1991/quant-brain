## Summary

Hazlitt argues that a minimum wage set above a worker's marginal productivity does not raise that worker's income — it prices them out of employment entirely, since an employer will not pay a wage exceeding the value the worker's labor adds. The visible effect is a wage floor for those who keep their jobs; the unseen effect is unemployment among the least productive workers whose marginal product falls below the mandated minimum.
