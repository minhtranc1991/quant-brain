## Summary

Hazlitt rebuts the fear that labor-saving machinery causes permanent net unemployment. Using the historical example of the power loom in the textile industry, he argues machinery lowers production costs, which lowers prices, which raises real purchasing power and demand elsewhere in the economy, redeploying (not permanently eliminating) labor — though he acknowledges real, painful short-run displacement for specific workers.
