## Summary

Hazlitt critiques agricultural price-support ("parity") schemes that fix farm prices relative to a historical base period, arguing that fixing relative prices ignores changed supply/demand conditions, produces chronic surpluses the government must then buy and store, and transfers cost to consumers and taxpayers rather than genuinely raising farmers' real income.
