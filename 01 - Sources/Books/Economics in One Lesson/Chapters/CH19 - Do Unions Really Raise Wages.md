# Chapter 19 — Do Unions Really Raise Wages?

## Summary

Hazlitt argues that unions can raise wages for their own members above the competitive level, but only by restricting entry into the trade or provoking substitution toward capital, which pushes displaced workers into other trades, depressing wages there — so union wage gains for insiders are partly offset by unseen wage effects on outsiders, and real wage growth for the economy as a whole ultimately depends on rising output per worker, not bargaining power.
