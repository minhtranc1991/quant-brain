## Summary

Hazlitt examines government commodity price-stabilization (buffer-stock) schemes, arguing that attempts to hold a commodity's price within a band regardless of actual supply/demand shifts tend to produce the same surplus-buying / shortage-selling problems as any price control, and that genuine price stabilization is better achieved by market speculation and storage responding to price signals than by a government agency overriding them.
