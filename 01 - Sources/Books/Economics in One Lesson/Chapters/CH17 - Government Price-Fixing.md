## Summary

Hazlitt argues that a legal price ceiling below the market-clearing price does not lower the real cost of a good — it removes the price signal that would otherwise ration scarce supply and call forth more production, producing shortages, queuing, black markets, and reduced future supply as producers exit rather than sell below cost; a price floor above market-clearing level produces the symmetric result of unsold surplus.
