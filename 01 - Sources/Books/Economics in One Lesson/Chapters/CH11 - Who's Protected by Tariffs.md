# Chapter 11 — Who's "Protected" by Tariffs?

## Summary

Hazlitt analyzes protective tariffs using the seen/unseen and opportunity-cost framework: a tariff visibly protects domestic producers and jobs in the protected industry, but raises prices to domestic consumers and reduces the sales (and jobs) of the more efficient foreign producers, and — because trade is at bottom an exchange of goods for goods — reduces foreign buyers' ability to purchase other domestic exports. The unseen cost is a less efficient allocation of capital and labor economy-wide than free trade would produce, paid for by consumers and unprotected export industries.
