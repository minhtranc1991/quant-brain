# Chapter 15 — How the Price System Works

## Summary

Hazlitt explains that the fundamental economic problem is allocating scarce labor and capital across competing wants — illustrated with a Robinson Crusoe example where every hour spent on one task is unavailable for another. In a modern economy this allocation problem is solved by the price system: prices, costs, and profits continuously signal where resources are most urgently wanted, reallocating capital and labor toward higher-valued uses and away from lower-valued ones without any central planner explicitly directing the process.
