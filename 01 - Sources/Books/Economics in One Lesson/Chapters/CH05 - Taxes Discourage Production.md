## Summary

Hazlitt argues that taxation, beyond funding public works, changes incentives at the margin: very high tax rates discourage the risk-taking, saving, and investment that fund private production, citing wartime top marginal rates as an extreme case. This is presented as a general incentive-effects argument rather than a market-mechanism claim.
