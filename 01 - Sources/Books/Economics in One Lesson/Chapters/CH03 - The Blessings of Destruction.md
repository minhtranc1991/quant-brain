# Chapter 03 — The Blessings of Destruction

## Summary

Hazlitt extends the broken-window fallacy to the claim that wars and natural disasters are economically beneficial because they stimulate post-destruction rebuilding demand. He argues this confuses need with effective demand backed by real resources: destroyed capital must be replaced out of savings and production that would otherwise have gone to raising living standards or expanding capital elsewhere, so aggregate wealth is lower than it would have been absent the destruction, even though the rebuilding activity is visible and war-era production statistics can look strong.
