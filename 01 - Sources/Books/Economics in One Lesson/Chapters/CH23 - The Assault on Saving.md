## Summary

Hazlitt defends saving and capital investment against the claim that thrift is economically harmful (the "paradox of thrift"): saved income that is lent or invested funds the purchase of capital goods and production just as directly as spent income funds consumption goods, so saving is not a leakage from the economy but a redirection of the same resources toward expanding future productive capacity — the opportunity cost of consuming now is forgone future output, and vice versa.
