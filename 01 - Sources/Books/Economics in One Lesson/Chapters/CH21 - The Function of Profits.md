## Summary

Hazlitt argues that profit and loss together perform an essential allocative function: profit signals that a firm is using resources in a way consumers value more than the resources cost, attracting more capital to that use, while loss signals resources are being wasted, forcing their reallocation — profits are therefore not simply a deduction from wages but the mechanism that continuously redirects capital toward its most valued uses.
