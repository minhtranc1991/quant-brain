## Summary

Introduces mental accounting (treating money differently depending on which mental 'account' it is assigned to, e.g. gambling winnings treated as more disposable than salary) and documents the sunk-cost fallacy and the related 'disposition effect' as consequences of a reluctance to close a mental account at a loss, which keeps the associated regret from being finalized.

Text: Mental accounting — the tendency to track money in separate, non-fungible psychological 'accounts' rather than as a single fungible pool (e.g. treating gambling winnings, or the 'house money' from an earlier win, as more disposable than equivalent salary income) — produces the sunk-cost fallacy (continuing a losing course of action, in part, to avoid the finality of closing that account at a realized loss) and reinforces the disposition effect already documented in this vault's [[C - Loss Aversion]] note: a paper loss can be held indefinitely partly because realizing it means closing (and finalizing the regret of) that specific mental account, not only because of the loss's raw financial magnitude.
