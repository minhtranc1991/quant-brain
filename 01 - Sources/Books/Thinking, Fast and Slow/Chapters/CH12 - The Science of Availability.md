# Chapter 12 — The Science of Availability

## Summary

Introduces the availability heuristic: people judge the frequency or probability of a category (e.g. causes of death, instances of one's own assertiveness) by how easily instances come to mind, which is affected by memorability and recency, not only true frequency.

Text: The availability heuristic — judging an event's frequency or probability by how easily instances come to mind, rather than by its actual base rate — is distorted by factors unrelated to true frequency (vividness, recency, personal involvement, media coverage), producing systematic over- or under-estimation of category frequencies (e.g. dramatic causes of death are recalled more easily, and judged more frequent, than statistically far more common but less memorable ones).
