## Summary

Distinguishes the 'inside view' (forecasting by focusing on the specific case's unique details and constructing a scenario) from the 'outside view' (forecasting by treating the case as one instance of a broader reference class of similar cases and using that class's actual statistical track record) — the inside view systematically produces the 'planning fallacy' (unrealistically optimistic timelines/budgets), which the outside view corrects.

## Claims

### Claim 1

Claim ID: `thinking-fast-and-slow__ch23-C001`

Text: Forecasts constructed from the 'inside view' — focusing on a specific case's unique circumstances and constructing a plausible scenario for it — are systematically more optimistic and less accurate than forecasts constructed from the 'outside view': identifying an appropriate reference class of similar past cases and using that class's actual statistical outcome distribution (e.g. base rates of cost/time overruns for similar past projects) as the starting forecast, adjusted only modestly for genuinely diagnostic case-specific information; the inside view's characteristic bias is the 'planning fallacy' — plans and forecasts unrealistically close to best-case scenarios, which the outside view's reference-class discipline directly corrects by anchoring on how similar past cases actually turned out rather than on how this particular case is imagined to unfold.
