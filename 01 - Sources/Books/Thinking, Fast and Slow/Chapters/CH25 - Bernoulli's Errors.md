# Chapter 25 — Bernoulli's Errors

## Summary

Explains the historical flaw in Daniel Bernoulli's 1738 expected-utility theory (later formalized by von Neumann-Morgenstern): utility is modeled as a function of final absolute wealth, which cannot explain why two people with identical final wealth but different starting wealth would evaluate an identical gamble differently — the theory has no concept of a reference point.

### Claim 1

Claim ID: `thinking-fast-and-slow__ch25-C001`

Fingerprint: `300b8365f8c9`

Text: Daniel Bernoulli's 1738 expected-utility theory (utility as a function of final absolute wealth) cannot explain reference-dependent risk attitudes: two individuals who end up with identical final wealth after a gamble, but who started from different wealth levels, are predicted by Bernoulli's theory to evaluate the outcome identically, which is empirically false — the theory has no mechanism for a reference point, and this blind spot went largely unaddressed for over two centuries until prospect theory's central innovation of reference-dependence.
