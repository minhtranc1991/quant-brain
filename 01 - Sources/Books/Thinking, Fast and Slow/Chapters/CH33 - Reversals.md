## Summary

Shows that preferences can reverse depending on whether options are evaluated jointly (side-by-side comparison) or singly (one at a time, out of context) — e.g. a chipped, larger dictionary can be preferred to a smaller, undamaged one when evaluated singly but not when compared jointly — a further violation of the rational-choice assumption of stable, context-independent preferences.
