# Chapter 24 — The Engine of Capitalism

## Summary

Documents the 'optimism bias' / entrepreneurial delusion: most entrepreneurs and business founders substantially overestimate their odds of success (competition neglect — underweighting how many similarly-confident competitors will fail) even as base-rate small-business failure statistics are widely known and even correctly cited by the same overconfident founders about the general population of businesses like their own.

## Claims

### Claim 1

Claim ID: `thinking-fast-and-slow__ch24-C001`

Fingerprint: `c694347aafe6`

Text: Optimism bias — a durable, largely unlearnable overestimation of one's own odds of success relative to others attempting similar ventures — is a leading cause of entrepreneurial and business risk-taking: founders routinely and correctly cite general small-business failure base rates for 'businesses like theirs' while simultaneously, and without contradiction, believing their own specific venture faces much better odds (competition neglect — underweighting the fact that competitors are, on average, equally skilled, equally resourced, and equally confident); Kahneman notes this bias-driven excess risk-taking is nonetheless economically productive in aggregate ('the engine of capitalism') even though it is costly and irrational for the great majority of individual overconfident founders who fail.
