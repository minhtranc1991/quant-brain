## Summary

Introduces the 'narrative fallacy' — coherent post-hoc stories about why past events happened (business-book case studies attributing a company's success to CEO traits that just as often preceded later failure) create an illusion that the past, and by extension the future, is more understandable and predictable than it really is, reinforced by hindsight bias's systematic revision of what 'was' predictable after the fact.

### Claim 1

Text: The 'narrative fallacy' — the mind's compulsion to construct coherent, causally satisfying stories about why past outcomes occurred (e.g. attributing a company's success entirely to its CEO's specific traits and decisions) — combines with hindsight bias (systematically revising, after the fact, one's sense of how predictable an outcome 'really' was) to create an illusion that past events were more understandable and future events are more predictable than the evidence actually supports; the same successful-company traits praised in retrospective business narratives are frequently found, on closer study, to characterize later-failing companies equally well, showing the narrative explains the outcome only because the outcome is already known.
