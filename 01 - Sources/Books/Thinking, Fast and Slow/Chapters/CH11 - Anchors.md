## Summary

Documents the anchoring effect: an arbitrary initial number (even one known to be random, e.g. a spun wheel-of-fortune result) measurably biases subsequent numerical estimates toward it, operating via both an insufficient-adjustment mechanism (System 2) and a priming/selective-accessibility mechanism (System 1).

Text: An arbitrary initial number presented immediately before a numerical estimation task — even one the subject knows to be random or irrelevant (e.g. a rigged spun wheel) — measurably biases the subsequent estimate toward it (the anchoring effect); the effect operates through two distinct mechanisms: insufficient adjustment away from an anchor considered as a starting hypothesis (an effortful, System-2-mediated process that people give up on too early), and priming/selective activation of anchor-compatible evidence (an automatic, System-1 process that operates even under time pressure or cognitive load).
