## Summary

Recommends adopting a broad decision frame — evaluating each risky choice as one instance of a rule or policy to be applied consistently across many similar decisions, rather than narrowly evaluating each decision in isolation — because narrow framing combined with loss aversion produces excessive, costly risk aversion in each individual decision, whereas broad framing (accepting the volatility of individual outcomes because the aggregate/expected outcome across many similar decisions is what matters) is closer to rational behavior and typically improves aggregate results.
