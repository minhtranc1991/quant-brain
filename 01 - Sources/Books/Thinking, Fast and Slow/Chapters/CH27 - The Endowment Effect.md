# Chapter 27 — The Endowment Effect

## Summary

Documents the endowment effect: once a person owns an object, they demand a substantially higher price to sell it (willingness-to-accept) than they would have been willing to pay to acquire the identical object beforehand (willingness-to-pay) — the classic 'mug experiment' — a distinct prediction of loss aversion (giving up an owned good is coded as a loss, more painful than the foregone-gain of not acquiring it) that does not apply to goods held purely for exchange/resale (e.g. a trader's inventory, or cash), where no such endowment gap is typically observed.
