## Summary

Kahneman and Gary Klein's joint 'adversarial collaboration' resolves apparent disagreement about expert intuition's validity: skilled intuition is genuinely trustworthy only in a sufficiently regular, predictable environment combined with prolonged practice and rapid, unambiguous feedback (e.g. chess, firefighting) — and is unreliable, however confident it feels, in low-validity or irregular environments (e.g. long-range political/economic forecasting, stock picking), where confidence and feedback delay make skill impossible to genuinely acquire.

### Claim 1

Text: Whether expert intuitive judgment can be trusted depends on two identifiable conditions, not on the expert's confidence or credentials: (1) the environment must be sufficiently regular/predictable to contain learnable patterns, and (2) the expert must have had prolonged practice with rapid, unambiguous feedback that allows genuine skill to be learned; when both conditions hold (chess, firefighting, some medical diagnosis), expert intuition can be genuinely reliable; when either is absent — as in stock picking and long-range political/economic forecasting, both low-validity, irregular environments with slow or ambiguous feedback — confident-feeling expert intuition is not evidence of genuine predictive skill, and the subjective confidence of an expert is not, by itself, a valid signal of which regime applies.
