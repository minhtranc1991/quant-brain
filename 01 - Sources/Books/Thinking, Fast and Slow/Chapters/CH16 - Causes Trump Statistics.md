## Summary

Shows that purely statistical base-rate information ('bare statistics') is routinely underweighted in judgment unless it is presented as a causal claim about an identifiable individual or a stereotyped subgroup, in which case it is over-applied indiscriminately (a stereotype) — statistical facts about a population are treated differently from a causal story about a case.
