Graham describes concrete enterprising strategies: relatively unpopular large companies, purchases below net current asset value ('net-nets'), special situations/arbitrage, and bargain issues generally.

Text: Buying a company's shares for less than its net current asset value (current assets minus all liabilities, ignoring fixed assets and goodwill entirely) provides a statistically demonstrable margin of safety and historically produced favorable aggregate results across diversified baskets of such 'net-net' issues, even though many individual constituent companies were mediocre businesses.
