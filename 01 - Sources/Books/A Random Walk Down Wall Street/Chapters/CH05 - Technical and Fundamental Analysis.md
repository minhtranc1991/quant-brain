## Summary

Introduces the two traditional schools Wall Street professionals use to try to beat the market: technical analysis (studying past price/volume patterns on the premise that history repeats) and fundamental analysis (estimating intrinsic value from earnings, growth, dividends, and interest rates, in the firm-foundation tradition). The chapter previews reasons both may fail: technical patterns may be self-defeating once known and heavily arbitraged, and fundamental analysis depends on forecasting future growth, which is inherently uncertain.
