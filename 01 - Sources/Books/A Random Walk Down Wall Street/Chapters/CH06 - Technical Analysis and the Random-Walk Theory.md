## Summary

Defines and tests the random-walk theory against technical analysis. Malkiel argues empirically that sequences of stock-price changes show no more persistence than sequences of coin flips: a classroom exercise where students construct 'stock charts' from coin-toss outcomes produces charts indistinguishable from real ones, including apparent head-and-shoulders and other chartist patterns. Various technical systems (filter rules, Dow theory, relative strength, and folk indicators such as the hemline and Super Bowl indicators) are reviewed and found not to survive transaction costs and taxes.
