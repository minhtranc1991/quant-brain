## Summary

Covers the Internet/dot-com bubble (1995-2000) and the U.S. housing bubble and crash (2000s): new-issue mania, dubious valuation metrics invented to justify unprofitable Internet companies, media complicity, accounting fraud (Enron, WorldCom), and the housing bubble driven by loosened lending standards and new securitized-banking structures. Malkiel asks whether these episodes mean markets are inefficient, previewing the EMH debate in Part Two.
