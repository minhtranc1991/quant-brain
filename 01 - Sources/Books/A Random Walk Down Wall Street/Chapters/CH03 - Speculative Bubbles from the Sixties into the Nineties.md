## Summary

Continues the historical survey of the castle-in-the-air theory in action: the 'Soaring Sixties' growth-stock/new-issue craze, the conglomerate boom ('synergy'), the concept-stock bubble, the Nifty Fifty of the early 1970s, the 1980s new-issue and biotechnology bubbles, the ZZZZ Best fraud, and the late-1980s Japanese asset bubble. A recurring case is Barry Minkow's ZZZZ Best -- a fraudulent carpet-cleaning company built on fabricated contracts -- illustrating that castle-in-the-air investing is also vulnerable to outright fraud, not only overoptimism.
