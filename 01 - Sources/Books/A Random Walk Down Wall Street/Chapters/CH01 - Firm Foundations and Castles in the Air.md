# Chapter 01 — Firm Foundations and Castles in the Air

## Summary

Malkiel introduces the book's central metaphor: stock valuation theories fall into two camps, the firm-foundation theory (a security has an intrinsic value based on discounted future dividends/earnings, and price gravitates toward it) and the castle-in-the-air theory (a security is worth whatever the crowd will believe it is worth, so successful investing means anticipating what other investors will believe, i.e. Keynes's newspaper beauty-contest logic). The chapter previews the book's argument that short-run stock-price changes are largely unpredictable (a 'random walk') and defines investing (long-horizon, dependable-return-seeking) versus speculating (short-horizon gain-seeking).
