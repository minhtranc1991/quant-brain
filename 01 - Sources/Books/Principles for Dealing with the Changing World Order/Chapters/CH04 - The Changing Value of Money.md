# Chapter 04 — The Changing Value of Money

## Summary

An extended appendix-style chapter arguing that every currency in recorded history has eventually been devalued or has died, and identifying the common empirical pattern behind a currency losing its reserve-currency status: an already-established relative economic/political decline versus a rising rival, large government debts increasingly monetized by central-bank money printing, and a resulting self-reinforcing 'run' out of the currency once fiscal and balance-of-payments deficits become too large to close through austerity. Dalio distinguishes ordinary currency devaluation (common, often survivable) from the much rarer loss of reserve-currency status (which requires chronic, large devaluation combined with a genuine loss of relative power), and notes falling central-bank reserves and suspended convertibility as observable leading indicators that typically precede a currency crisis by months to years.
