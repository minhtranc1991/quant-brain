# Chapter 03 — The Big Cycle of Money, Credit, Debt, and Economic Activity

## Summary

The book's core money-and-debt mechanism chapter. Dalio argues every entity (person, company, government) faces the same income/expense/savings/debt arithmetic, and that debt is simply a claim on future money that must eventually be repaid, converted, or defaulted on. He lays out a six-stage long-term debt cycle running from hard (intrinsic-value) money, through claims on hard money, through increased debt, to debt crises/defaults/devaluations, to fiat money, and eventually back to a flight into hard money — a cycle he argues has repeated across every monetary system in recorded history.

## Claims

### Claim 1

Claim ID: `principles-for-dealing-with-the-changing-world-order__ch03-C001`

Fingerprint: `b52e9da26567`

Text: The long-term debt cycle has a repeating six-stage structure: (1) it begins with no or low debt and 'hard money' backed by something with intrinsic value (e.g. gold/silver); (2) claims on hard money ('notes'/paper money) are introduced as a more convenient substitute; (3) debt then increases as credit expands faster than hard-money backing; (4) this produces debt crises, defaults, and devaluations once debt service exceeds the capacity of income/hard-money reserves to support it; (5) governments respond by moving fully to fiat money (money with no intrinsic value, backed only by trust/decree), which allows debt and money supply to expand further; (6) that expansion eventually triggers a flight back into hard money as trust in fiat money erodes, restarting the cycle.

### Claim 2

Claim ID: `principles-for-dealing-with-the-changing-world-order__ch03-C002`

Fingerprint: `8efdd9534c2b`

Text: Printing/devaluing money is systematically the path of least political resistance out of a debt crisis compared to austerity or outright default, because it is less visible and less immediately painful to the electorate, even though it functions as a real wealth transfer from holders of money/debt (creditors, cash savers) to debtors — this is why chronic currency devaluation, not formal default, is the dominant historical resolution mechanism for sovereign debt crises.
